Check Before You Pay

Most hostel scams start with one request: money before you have seen anything. Use the steps below to visit safely, and learn the signs of a fake agent.

Never pay before viewing: steps for a safe visit

Follow these steps in order. Do not pay anything until you finish step 7.

  1. Start with a trusted source. Ask VU's Dean of Students for advice, or choose from the hostels we checked on the Find a Hostel page. Do not rely only on a stranger who messages you.
  2. Book a daytime visit. Agree the date, time and address with the manager before you go.
  3. Tell someone where you are going. Send a friend or family member the hostel name, the address and the time of your visit.
  4. Do not go alone. Take a friend, a classmate or a student who already lives there.
  5. Meet the manager or owner at the hostel. Write down their full name and phone number. Ask how long they have run the hostel.
  6. See the room you will really get. Check the lock, the window, the water, the power, the fence, the guard and the fire exit. A photo is not enough.
  7. Ask students who live there. Check that the price, the rules and the security are what the manager told you. Then go home and decide without pressure.

First visit rule: pay nothing on the first visit, not even a small "booking fee".

Warning signs of a fake agent or broker

One sign is a reason to slow down. Two or more is a reason to walk away.

  • Asks for a deposit or "reservation fee" before you have seen the room.
  • Says "only one room left" or "pay today or lose it" to make you hurry.
  • Will not meet you at the hostel, or wants to meet on the roadside or in a car.
  • Asks you to send mobile money to a personal number, or to a different name each time.
  • Offers a price far lower than similar hostels near VU.
  • Shows photos that do not match the room you are shown.
  • Says they work for the university or the hostel, but cannot prove it or give the manager's number.
  • Refuses to let you speak to students who live there.
  • Refuses to give a receipt or a written agreement.

Already sent money to someone suspicious? Stop all further payments and read what to do first on the Help & Contact page.

Receipts and a written agreement

Never pay without paper. A good receipt and agreement protect you if there is a dispute.

What a receipt must show
  • Your full name and the hostel name
  • Amount paid and what it is for
  • Date, and the signature and phone number of the receiver
  • For mobile money: keep the confirmation SMS and check the name shown
What a written agreement must show
  • Room number, rent per semester or month, and due dates
  • Deposit amount and the conditions for getting it back
  • House rules, visitor rules and notice period
  • Signatures of both you and the manager

Take a photo of every receipt and agreement and send it to a family member.
